Many Raft groups, one cluster

Multi-Raft in Motion

← Single-group Raft simulator

One Raft group can only hold so much data and push so many writes through one leader. Systems like TiKV and CockroachDB cut the key space into regions and run a separate Raft group for each one. Five stores below host many groups at once. Each group elects its own leader. A placement driver spreads leaders and replicas, splits regions that grow too big, and repairs groups when a store dies.

What just happened

    Sharding

    Key space 0–999, cut into regions

    Each segment is one region: a key range with its own Raft group. Width is the share of the key space. Clients look up the region for a key and send the write to that region's leader.

    Why many groups

    A single Raft group sends every write through one leader and stores every byte on every member. Splitting the data into regions lets each store lead some groups and follow others, so write load and storage spread over the whole cluster. A failure only affects the groups that had a replica on the failed store.

    What each store must do

    • Batch messages. Hundreds of groups between the same two stores would mean hundreds of tiny packets. Stores coalesce them into one.
    • Route by region. Every message carries a region id, so the receiving store hands it to the right replica, creating an empty one if a leader asks.
    • Compact logs. Old entries are dropped once applied. A replica that falls too far behind gets a snapshot instead.

    What the placement driver does

    • Balances leaders with leader transfer (TimeoutNow), so no store does all the write work.
    • Repairs a group that lost a replica: add a learner elsewhere, send it a snapshot, promote it, then remove the dead replica. One membership change at a time.
    • Splits big regions at the median key. The new region starts its own Raft group on the same stores.

    Try this

    • Select the store with the most leaders and crash it. Only the groups it led hold elections, all at once. After 1.5 simulated seconds the placement driver starts rebuilding the missing replicas elsewhere.
    • Turn batching off and watch packets per second jump to match Raft messages per second. Turn it back on and they collapse into a handful of packets per store pair.
    • Turn pre-vote off, isolate a store for a few seconds, then heal it. Its groups come back with inflated terms and force healthy leaders to step down. With pre-vote on, nothing happens.

    The safety chip checks, per region, that no two leaders share a term and that committed entries never change. Membership changes add or remove one voter at a time, so no joint consensus is needed.

    Inspired by TiKV's regions and placement driver and CockroachDB's ranges. Each group follows the Raft paper (Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014) plus pre-vote, check-quorum, leader transfer, learners and snapshots from the Raft thesis.
